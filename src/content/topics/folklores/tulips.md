Farhad was a talented stonemason who fell deeply in love with Shirin, a princess renowned for her beauty. Their love was considered impossible. 

## The Legend

According to the legend, Farhdad was given an enormous challenge: "he would be allowed to marry Shirin only if he could carve through and entire mountain and create a channel that would bring warer to the people. <br>
Farhad accepted. Day after day, year after year, he chipped away at the mountain. His determination came directly from the love he had for Shirin.<br>
As the story goes, those who wanted to keep then apart grew worried that he might actually succees. So they devised a cruel plan. <br>
They sent a messenger to him who informed him that Shirin had died.<br>
The news shattered him. 

## The Tragedy

Believing he had lost the only reason he endured such harshdip, he threw himself from the mountain and died. When Shirin learned what happened, she was overcome with grief and according to some versions she also died later.


## The Birth Of Tulip

Persian folklore says that where Farhad's blood touched the ground, red tulips sprang from the Earth.


## Symbol

Because of this, the red tulip became a symbol of <i>passionate love, sacrifice, devotion and heartbreak.</i><br>
In Persian poetry, a field of red tulips often represents love so powerful that it survives even death.

## Why it sticks

This story really isn't about whether Farhad could move a mountain or not. <br> It is about what happens when a person devotes themselves completely to something they love.<br> Farhad never stopped because the task was difficult.<br> He stopped only when he believed the meaning behind it was gone.<br> This is why the tragedy feels timeless.<br> The mountain wasn't what defeated him.<br><i> Despair did</i>
