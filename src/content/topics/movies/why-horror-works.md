Humans have a strange hobby.

We deliberately consume stories designed to make us afraid.

We watch monsters, ghosts, killers, disasters, and things lurking in dark rooms.

Then we complain that we couldn't sleep.

So why do we do it?

## Fear without actual danger

Horror allows us to experience fear while remaining relatively safe.

Your brain can react to frightening images and sounds even when another part of you knows you're sitting on a couch watching a movie.

The body reacts.

Heart rate increases.

Muscles tense.

Attention narrows.

But once the threat disappears, the tension can become relief.

## Anticipation

One of horror's most powerful tools is anticipation.

Sometimes the monster is less frightening when you actually see it.

A dark hallway can be scarier when you don't know what's inside it.

The human brain is extremely good at predicting threats.

Give it incomplete information and it starts filling in the gaps.

Usually with something horrible.

## The social side

Watching horror with other people changes the experience too.

Fear becomes something shared.

People scream together, laugh afterward, and talk about the scariest scene.

That mixture of fear, excitement, relief, and social connection can make horror surprisingly enjoyable.

The best horror doesn't simply show you something frightening.

It makes your own imagination do most of the work.
