A great plot twist doesn't simply surprise you.

It makes you reinterpret what you've already seen.

That's why the best twists stay in your head long after the movie ends.

## Surprise isn't enough

If a character suddenly turns out to be an alien with absolutely no setup, that's technically surprising.

It just isn't satisfying.

A good twist usually contains information that was visible earlier but interpreted incorrectly.

The audience had the pieces.

They just didn't know how they fit together.

## Misdirection

Filmmakers can manipulate attention by giving certain details more importance than others.

A suspicious object might be placed in the background.

A character might say something that sounds meaningless.

A camera shot might linger slightly longer than expected.

You notice the information without understanding why it matters.

Later, the reveal changes its meaning.

## The second viewing

This is where great twists become particularly interesting.

Once you know the ending, the movie becomes a different experience.

Scenes that originally looked ordinary suddenly become evidence.

A line of dialogue becomes suspicious.

A character's expression makes sense.

The movie hasn't changed.

Your interpretation has.

That's the real power of a plot twist.

It doesn't just change the story.

It changes the story you thought you had already watched.
