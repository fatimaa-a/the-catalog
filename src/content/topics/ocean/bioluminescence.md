Imagine living somewhere where sunlight never reaches.

You can't rely on vision the way surface animals do.

So what do you do?

You make your own light.

That's bioluminescence.

## Living lights

Bioluminescence is the production of light by living organisms through chemical reactions.

It appears across an astonishing range of life.

Jellyfish, squid, fish, fungi, insects, and microscopic organisms can all produce light.

## Why do it?

Different organisms use it for different reasons.

Some use light to attract prey.

Some use it to communicate.

Some use it to confuse predators.

Others use it as camouflage.

One particularly strange strategy is called the "burglar alarm" effect.

Some animals release glowing material when attacked, essentially making themselves visible in the hope that a larger predator will attack whatever is attacking them.

The ocean is full of strategies like this.

And we've probably discovered only a fraction of them.
