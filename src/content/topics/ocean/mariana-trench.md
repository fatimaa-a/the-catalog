The Mariana Trench is the deepest known part of Earth's oceans.

At its deepest point, Challenger Deep, the seafloor lies almost eleven kilometres beneath the surface.

Mount Everest could fit inside it with room to spare.

## A completely different world

Down there, sunlight doesn't reach.

The pressure is enormous.

Temperatures hover only slightly above freezing.

And yet life exists there.

Tiny organisms have evolved to survive conditions that would be lethal to humans almost instantly.

## Why explore it?

We've mapped large portions of Mars more thoroughly than parts of our own deep ocean.

The deep sea remains one of Earth's great unknown environments.

Every expedition has the potential to reveal organisms, ecosystems, geological structures, or chemical processes we've never seen before.

And that's what makes the trench so fascinating.

It's not another planet.

It's right here.
